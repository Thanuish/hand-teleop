Before you start

  • A computer with a webcam (laptop or desktop). The test takes about 20 minutes.
  • A recent Chrome or Edge works best. Firefox and Safari work, more slowly on older computers.
  • Plug in the charger: on battery, many laptops slow down and the hand tracking gets choppy.
  • Close video calls and other heavy apps or tabs: they compete for the camera and the graphics chip.
  • No webcam? You can use a phone as the camera: the start screen shows a QR code for it.

Allowing the camera

When you press Start the camera, the browser asks for permission. Choose Allow.

If you blocked it by mistake

  • Chrome / Edge: click the camera or lock icon at the left of the address bar, set Camera to Allow, reload the page.
  • Safari (Mac): Safari menu → Settings for this website → Camera: Allow, reload.
  • Firefox: click the camera icon in the address bar, remove the block, reload.

"Camera in use" or a black picture

  • Another app has the camera (Teams, Zoom, Skype...). Quit it and reload.
  • Laptops with a privacy shutter or a camera key: open it / switch it on.
  • Windows: Settings → Privacy & security → Camera: allow apps and your browser to use it. Mac: System Settings → Privacy & Security → Camera: tick your browser.

Light and position

  • Light: face a window or a lamp so your hand is lit from the front. A bright window behind you makes your hand dark and the tracking unsteady.
  • Background: a plain wall or desk behind your hand is best; avoid busy patterns at the height of your hand.
  • Distance: sit about an arm's length from the camera. The setup screen tells you to move closer or back.
  • The box: the dashed box on the camera picture is where your hand should stay. Near its edge the tracking loses fingers, so the box turns amber; if your hand leaves it, the robot pauses until you bring it back (no harm done).
  • Your hand: palm toward the camera, fingers relaxed and spread a little. Rest your elbow on the desk: your hand gets steadier and you tire less.

The see-through gripper shows straight away where the robot is going; the robot follows it a moment later. The ring on the table under the gripper turns green when it is low enough to grab.

Jittery or slow?

  • Shaky robot: usually poor light or a busy background. Turn on a light in front of you, rest your elbow, keep your hand in the middle of the box.
  • Robot moves when you move toward / away from the screen: that's the camera's hardest direction. Move calmly, and use a fist to reposition.
  • "Only N pictures a second" on the setup screen: close other apps and tabs, plug in the charger, use Chrome or Edge. If it stays slow, choose Track on our server (see privacy), or continue anyway: it will feel less smooth.
  • The hand isn't found: more light, palm facing the camera, the whole hand (wrist to fingertips) inside the box.
  • The pinch doesn't register: bring the tips of thumb and index finger clearly together, keep the other fingers relaxed. Or use SPACE.
  • The page lost the robot (red dot top right): your connection dropped. It reconnects by itself; if not, reload the page: you continue where you were.

Privacy

The camera picture is processed in your browser: only the position of your hand (21 points) is sent to the test. The video is not recorded or uploaded. Your control of the robot (positions, times, grips) is recorded and scored for your application.

Only if your computer is too slow and you choose Track on our server on the setup screen: the camera pictures are sent to the test's server, used there only to find your hand, and never stored.
